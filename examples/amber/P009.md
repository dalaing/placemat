# P009 on Amber: hot-code ordering strategies compared

Apple M2, macOS, Apple clang 17, ld64; Amber upstream main d596e57a (`trees/A` of the regression test), portable `-O3 -flto` build. 2026-10-05.

**Question** (ISSUES.md P009, DESIGN §6.4): does it matter *which* ordering of the hot set Amber pins? The pinned build C2 used C3. Here five strategies (c3, hotness, density, Pettis-Hansen, and random order as a control, with two seeds) each get the full pin procedure. Then each is measured against the stock build, averaged over layouts of its own pinned arrangement rather than judged from one lucky build.

## Procedure

Inputs: the stored profile `planning/amber-validation/inputs/prof2.json` (126 maintainer cases and 130 of ours, two benchmark sets weighted equally) and selection `inputs/sel.json` (101 functions: 99.3% / 95.2% of in-binary samples). Hot entry spans come from `select.hot_spans` on the profiled binary (75 spans). They are identical to the stored `inputs/hotspans.json`.

Static half: `placemat pin compare` (new, `placemat/pin/compare.py`). For each strategy it:
1. orders the hot set on the stock binary (`order.strategies`) and writes the order file;
2. builds through Amber's adapter with `PLACEMAT_ORDER_FILE` (the adapter adds `-falign-functions=16` and `-Wl,-order_file`), no pads;
3. plans pads on that build (`pads.pads`, C2's rule: no small loop of a hot function and no hot entry span across 4 KB; weights 10⁶/10⁴/–/1);
4. rebuilds with the padded order file and the pad functions (`PLACEMAT_PIN_SOURCE`);
5. verifies with `verify(plan=...)`.

```
cd ../amber && PYTHONPATH=../placemat python3.12 -m placemat pin compare \
  ../placemat/planning/amber-validation/inputs/prof2.json ../placemat/planning/amber-validation/inputs/sel.json \
  --config pbt/placemat/placemat.toml --source ../placemat-work/trees/A --work ../placemat-work \
  --binary <stock build of A> -o ../placemat-work/p009/strategies
```

Measured half: one multi-arm `placemat run`. The base is the stock build of main; each strategy is an arm (`--arm NAME=trees/A --order NAME=…/pinned.order --pin-source NAME=…/pads.c`) with `--pad-first --data none`. With `--pad-first` the code-axis pad is listed first in each pinned arm's order file, so every designed pad moves the whole ordered region (pads included) as one block. Each arm's interval therefore covers layouts of its pinned arrangement. The stock arm's pads move everything after `src/0.c`'s top, as usual. Run script: `../placemat-work/p009/run.sh`.

**Check:** the c3 arm reproduces build C2 exactly: the same order file, the same 18 pads (3504 bytes), the same text size (624784), and 119 of 119 ordered symbols at C2's addresses (the adapter was checked against the C2 tree before installing).

## Static measures

Text: the `__text` section of the verified pinned build. Pages: the hot code's footprint in 4 KB and 16 KB pages, as *touched / p90 / p99*:
- touched: pages holding any sampled hot code;
- p90 / p99: the fewest pages holding 90% / 99% of the hot samples, profile-weighted with each set counting equally and each function's weight spread over its sampled offsets.

"Effective" pages (exp of the entropy of the page weights) are in `compare.json`. Crossings: small loops (≤256 B) of the 101 hot functions / hot entry spans straddling a 4 KB boundary, before padding (ordered, aligned, no pads) and after (the verified build).

| strategy | text bytes | vs stock | pads | pad bytes | hot region | 4 KB pages | 16 KB pages | eff. 4K / 16K | crossings before | after | verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stock (unordered) | 617548 | +0 | – | – | (spread over 606 KB) | 75 / 38 / 64 | 33 / 17 / 27 | 37.4 / 18.4 | 27 / 11 | – | – |
| c3 (= C2) | 624784 | +7236 | 18 | 3504 | 215120 | 45 / 22 / 37 | 13 / 7 / 11 | 23.0 / 7.0 | 26 / 5 | 0 / 0 | OK |
| hotness | 624704 | +7156 | 19 | 3424 | 215040 | 44 / 26 / 38 | 14 / 8 / 12 | 27.2 / 9.6 | 24 / 6 | 0 / 0 | OK |
| density | 626288 | +8740 | 19 | 5008 | 216624 | 46 / 23 / 39 | 13 / 7 / 11 | 19.1 / 6.2 | 37 / 5 | 0 / 0 | OK |
| pettis_hansen | 624272 | +6724 | 14 | 2992 | 214608 | 47 / 26 / 41 | 14 / 10 / 13 | 28.2 / 10.7 | 35 / 7 | 0 / 0 | OK |
| random-1 | 625680 | +8132 | 18 | 4400 | 216016 | 47 / 28 / 41 | 14 / 10 / 12 | 31.0 / 11.6 | 24 / 8 | 0 / 0 | OK |
| random-2 | 626896 | +9348 | 18 | 5616 | 217232 | 46 / 27 / 40 | 14 / 10 / 12 | 29.4 / 10.6 | 20 / 6 | 0 / 0 | OK |

Reading the table:
- **Any ordering packs the hot code; which ordering matters less.** Ordering alone (even random) gathers the 101 hot functions into 215 KB. The 16 KB-page footprint falls from 33 touched pages to 13-14, and the pages holding 99% of the samples from 27 to 11-13. Between strategies, the measures that do differ are the weighted ones: 90% of samples sit in 7 16 KB pages under c3 and density, against 10 under random and Pettis-Hansen, and 8 under hotness. density is the most concentrated (19 effective 4 KB pages, against 23 for c3 and 29-31 for random). That is what density sorting optimises.
- **Pettis-Hansen packs no better than random here.** Our implementation lays its chains out by density but joins along call edges first, so cold callers end up next to hot callees. With only 101 functions and a flat profile, call-graph locality buys nothing in pages.
- **Crossings before padding are every layout's luck,** 20-37 loops and 5-8 spans, and the pad search removes all of them for every strategy. Its cost is 3.0-5.6 KB of pads, with the random orders paying the most. Every verified build has 0 residual crossings.
- Text grows by 6.7-9.3 KB over stock (alignment gaps plus pads), about 1.1-1.5%.

Files: `../placemat-work/p009/strategies/<strategy>/{order,pinned.order,pads.c,plan.json,verify.txt}`, `compare.{json,md}`, `spans.json`.

## Measured comparison

Run `p009` (`../placemat-work/p009/p009.{md,json,raw.json}`; per-strategy summary `summary.md`, from `raw.json` by a scratch script). Seven arms: stock as the base, plus the six pinned strategies. Data axis skipped; 30 cases; 12 designed pads (8, then 4 more, cap 12; every case reached 12); 7 rounds per batch. The cases:
- the cases main code-flags in the reference runs: the setdictbig family (5), chargrade, takekeys10x10;
- the cases a pinned layout's luck moved in the order-file report: grade, igradedown, dategrade, stampgrade, jsonread, jsonshort, jsonints, datelist;
- the parse- and interpreter-heavy ones: jsonread17, jsonwrite, parse, fparse17, fparseshort, eachenum, kprint, setdictnest;
- controls: fsum, sc_member, s_grp10, qgroup, qasc, qlj, qhhtime.

Every padded build of every pinned arm was checked after the run (symbol table): the ordered region keeps its planned internal layout and moves only as a whole, by the pad's footprint mod 4096 (12 offsets: 80 … 3792 bytes). The adapter also checks the order of every order-file entry after each link.

**Conditions: a noisy machine.** Six placemat runs shared the machine lock. Batch 1 ended at load 8.1 (noise probe 6.2%) and ran about 1.6× slower than batch 0 for *every* arm alike, and within-round variation was about 15% (S1 ≈ 0.02-0.09). The paired per-round ratios cancel that drift, so the change intervals are usable (±1.5-5% per case). The code spreads (range of the 12 pads' medians) are not: 20-28% median for every arm, the stock arm included, with no code flag anywhere. This run cannot separate the strategies by spread. (The designed pads also missed stock setdictbig's rare 2× window, which the regression test's targeted pads catch.)

**No strategy is distinguishable from another.** Every pairwise interval against c3 contains zero. The two random orders sit inside the spread of the four real strategies, and c3 (the C2 build) is neither the best nor the worst. Pinning in general is neutral to slightly faster on this case set: −0.4% to −1.8% against stock, as C2's −0.6% over 200 cases in the order-file report. Only Pettis-Hansen's interval excludes zero, and only just. With six arms and a noisy run, that is not evidence that Pettis-Hansen is better.

  - random-1: jsonread17 −3.1% [−6.0, −0.1].
- **Largest point slowdowns**, all with intervals containing zero or under the 3% threshold:
  - random-1: chargrade +4.0% [−4.0, +12.6];
  - hotness: sc_member +2.3% [−0.2, +4.8];
  - c3: qlj +2.2%;
  - density: qhhtime +2.2%;
  - random-2: setdictbigall +2.3%, qasc +2.1% [+0.3, +3.9].
- **Front-end-heavy cases** (json, parse, eachenum, kprint): every strategy is within −4.3% … +2.0%. No strategy loses on any of them. Packing differences (density's 19 effective 4 KB pages against random's 29-31) do not show.
- **The q cases** (qgroup, qasc, qlj, qhhtime) are +0-2% slower for most strategies, random included. They are consistent across arms, so this is probably the pinned builds' own small cost (alignment and pads), not an ordering effect; it is under threshold everywhere.

Each strategy against c3, per case (paired, >3% and interval excluding zero): only hotness is faster, on jsonread (−3.7%). Nothing is slower.

Judged from one build each, density and Pettis-Hansen would look like clear wins on the parse cases, and random-1 like a 4-8% loss on four cases. Averaged over the region's position, all of those shrink to within ±3% and mostly inside the noise. Each pinned arrangement freezes its own luck, as P009 warned (the order-file report saw ±5-10% per pinned build). The `--pad-first` design is what keeps this comparison honest. (These planned-layout intervals are over 7 rounds of a noisy run, so some of these are themselves noise.)

## Against DESIGN's expectation

ISSUES.md P009 expected **small differences on Amber/M2**: instruction-fetch stalls are about 6% of the maintainer's set, packing the hot code changed none of the front-end-heavy cases measurably, and the L1 instruction cache is large. **Confirmed.**
- The strategies differ statically in how tightly they pack the hot samples. density and c3 hold 90% of samples in 7 16 KB pages; random and Pettis-Hansen need 10.
- They differ in pad cost (3.0-5.6 KB) and in crossings before padding (20-37 loops).
- After the pad rule, all have 0 crossings, and the measured speeds agree within ±1.5% (geomean). No case shows a strategy effect beyond the run's noise.
- Random order, the control, is as good as any. On M2, with this hot set (101 functions, 215 KB), the order inside the pinned region does not matter measurably. What matters is that it is pinned and padded. The pad rule removes the one hazard known to cost 4-10% (loops or entry spans across 4 KB), and the pinned region's position matters case by case (the table above).

**Recommendation for Amber:** keep c3 (C2's order). Nothing beats it measurably, and changing it would only reshuffle which few cases carry a frozen ±5% of luck. A strategy comparison is worth re-running where P006 lands: x86, with a 32 KB L1I and fetch-window boundaries. There the packing differences in the static table (the pages holding 90% of samples) could become visible.

**Limits of this run:**
- One run, on a loaded machine (batch 1 at load 8).
- 30 cases chosen for sensitivity, not the full set.
- 12 pads per arm; designed pads only, so no targeted pads for rare windows.
- One profile.
- Random order measured with two seeds only.

A quiet replicate (different `--seed`) would tighten the ±1.5-2% geomean intervals. It would mostly matter for Pettis-Hansen's borderline −1.8%.
